<Objectives>

- Write **attributes** and **operations** with visibility, types and default values.
- Choose the right **relationship**: association, aggregation, composition, generalization, dependency or realization.
- Add **multiplicities** and **role names** to associations.
- Turn a **requirements text** into a class diagram, and the diagram into code.

</Objectives>

## Classes

A **class** describes a set of objects with similar attributes, operations, relationships and behaviour. It is drawn as a rectangle with **three compartments**: the **name**, the **attributes** (data) and the **operations** (methods).

<SlideFigure page={17} caption="The Shape class: +origin is public, #width: int is protected, and -height: int = 10 is private with an initial value." />

```text title="Syntax"
attribute:  visibility name : type = initialValue        e.g.  -height : int = 10
operation:  visibility name(param : type = default) : returnType
                                                        e.g.  -display(c1 : boolean = true) : boolean
visibility: + public    # protected    - private    (~ package)
```

<SlideFigure page={18} caption="Operations of Drawing::Shape: +move(), #resize(): boolean, -display(c1: boolean = true): boolean." />

Attribute and operation names start with a **lowercase** letter. Class names start with an **uppercase** letter and are singular nouns.

## Relationships

<SlideFigure page={19} caption="The six kinds of relationship between classes." />

| Relationship | Notation | Read as | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Association** | Solid line, with optional name, arrow, multiplicities and role names | "is connected to" | Student — Instructor |
| **Aggregation** | Hollow diamond at the **whole** | "has", where the parts can live alone | Class ◇— Student |
| **Composition** | Filled diamond at the **whole** | "is made of", where the parts die with the whole | House ◆— Room |
| **Generalization** | Hollow triangle at the **parent** | "is a" | Student ▷ Person |
| **Dependency** | Dashed arrow | "uses" (temporarily) | Accounting System ⇢ Receipt |
| **Realization** | Dashed line with a hollow triangle | "implements" | ArrayList ⇢▷ List |

### Association, multiplicity and role names

When two classes need to communicate, there must be an **association** between them. An arrow shows the navigation direction; arrows at both ends (or none) mean a **bidirectional** association.

<SlideFigure page={21} caption="Every Instructor has one or more Students; with role names, an Instructor 'teaches' Students and a Student 'learns from' Instructors." />

Read a multiplicity from the **far** end: in `Student 1..* ——— 1..* Instructor`, *one* instructor has **1..\*** students, and *one* student has **1..\*** instructors.

### Aggregation vs. composition

Both are special **associations** where one object "owns" another. The difference is whether the part can **exist independently**:

| | Aggregation | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| Part without the whole? | **Yes**. Delete the *Class* and the *Students* still exist | **No**. Delete the *House* and its *Rooms* are gone |
| Diamond | Hollow ◇ | Filled ◆ |

<SlideFigure page={23} caption="Composition: a Person is composed of a Head, Hands and Legs; a coffee table of a table top and legs." />

<SlideFigure page={24} caption="Aggregation: a Car aggregates an Engine and Wheels (which an engine inspection can also refer to)." />

### Generalization and dependency

**Generalization** (inheritance): a class with no parent is a **root** class, one with no children is a **leaf** class, and a class can have **single** or **multiple** inheritance. A **dependency** means one class **uses** another, e.g. as a parameter or a local variable, without keeping a permanent link.

<SlideFigure page={26} caption="Dependency: the Accounting System uses Receipt (dashed arrow)." />

## Worked example: the researcher database

> Researchers are placed into a database maintained by the state of Georgia. Information of interest includes **researcher name, title, position, date began current position, number of years at current position**; **university name, location, enrollment**; and **research interests**. Researchers are associated with **one institution**, and each researcher can have **up to five** research interests. **More than one researcher** can have the same interest, and the system tracks the **ranking of the best researchers** for each interest. The system should be able to insert new researchers, universities and interests; produce the **number of researchers at each university**, **contact information** for researchers, and **interests with no associated researchers**; and **change researcher rankings**.

<Steps>

1. **Nouns become classes**: *Institution*, *Researcher*, *Research Interest*.
2. **Facts about each noun become attributes**:
   - Institution: name, location, enrollment.
   - Researcher: name, title, position, date of current position, and **/years at current position**. The leading **/** marks a **derived** attribute, computed from the date.
   - Interest: description, top researcher.
3. **"The system should produce/change…" becomes operations on the class that owns the data**:
   - `numberOfResearchers()` on Institution;
   - `produceContactInfo()` on Researcher;
   - `findNoAssociatedResearcher()` and `changeResearcherRanking()` on Interest.
4. **Relationships and multiplicities come from the quantity words**:
   - "associated with **one** institution": Institution **1** — **1..\*** Researcher;
   - "**up to five** interests" and "**more than one** researcher can share an interest": Researcher **1..\*** — **1..5** Research Interest.

</Steps>

<SlideFigure page={18} source={1} caption="The solution: Institution 1 —associated— 1..* Researcher 1..* —has— 1..5 Research Interests, with their attributes and operations." />

The diagram maps directly to code:

```java title="Researchers.java"
import java.time.LocalDate;
import java.time.Period;
import java.util.ArrayList;
import java.util.List;

class Institution {
    private String name, location;
    private int enrollment;
    private final List<Researcher> researchers = new ArrayList<>();   // 1 —— 1..*

    int numberOfResearchers() { return researchers.size(); }
}

class Researcher {
    private String name, title, position;
    private LocalDate dateCurrentPosition;
    private Institution institution;                                  // exactly 1
    private final List<ResearchInterest> interests = new ArrayList<>(); // 1..5

    int yearsCurrentPosition() {                                      // derived: /Years Curr.
        return Period.between(dateCurrentPosition, LocalDate.now()).getYears();
    }

    void addInterest(ResearchInterest i) {
        if (interests.size() == 5) throw new IllegalStateException("at most 5 interests");
        interests.add(i);
    }

    String produceContactInfo() { return name + ", " + title; }
}

class ResearchInterest {
    private String description;
    private final List<Researcher> ranking = new ArrayList<>();       // best first

    Researcher topResearcher() { return ranking.isEmpty() ? null : ranking.get(0); }
    boolean hasNoAssociatedResearcher() { return ranking.isEmpty(); }
    void changeResearcherRanking(Researcher r, int newRank) {
        ranking.remove(r);
        ranking.add(newRank, r);
    }
}
```

<Callout type="note" title="Multiplicities become code constraints">
  <code>1..5</code> isn't only a label: <code>addInterest</code> enforces the upper bound. A lower bound such as <code>1..*</code> means a researcher must have at least one interest, which the application should check when saving.
</Callout>

<Takeaways>

- A class has **name, attributes and operations**, with visibility `+ # - ~`, types and default values.
- Use **association** to connect classes, with **multiplicities** read from the far end and optional **role names**.
- **Aggregation** ◇: the part can live alone. **Composition** ◆: the part dies with the whole. **Generalization** ▷: is-a. **Dependency** ⇢: uses.
- From requirements: **nouns become classes**, their **facts become attributes**, **"the system should…" becomes operations**, and **quantity words become multiplicities**.

</Takeaways>

## Practice

<Exercise title="Online shop" level="medium">

A customer places orders. Each order has one or more order lines, and each line refers to exactly one product with a quantity. An order line can't exist without its order. Products exist independently and appear in many lines. Customers are either regular or premium (premium customers get a discount). Draw the class diagram as a list of classes, attributes and relationships.

<Solution>

- **Customer** (name, email) 1 —places— 0..\* **Order** (orderId, date; `total()`).
- **Order** ◆—1..\* **OrderLine** (quantity; `subtotal()`). This is **composition**: lines die with their order.
- **OrderLine** 0..\* —refers to— 1 **Product** (name, price). This is a plain **association**, because products exist independently.
- **RegularCustomer** and **PremiumCustomer** (discountRate) **generalize** to **Customer** (hollow triangle at Customer).

</Solution>

</Exercise>

<Exercise title="Name the relationship" level="easy">

1. A *Library* and its *Books* (books can be moved to another library).
2. A *Document* and its *Pages*.
3. A *ReportGenerator* that receives a *Printer* as a method parameter.
4. A *Manager* and an *Employee* (every manager is an employee).

<Solution>

1. Aggregation (hollow diamond at Library).
2. Composition (filled diamond at Document).
3. Dependency (dashed arrow from ReportGenerator to Printer).
4. Generalization (Manager ▷ Employee).

</Solution>
